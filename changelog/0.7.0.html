RIS 0.7.0

← changelog · older: 0.6.7

What's changed (v. 0.7.0)

  • Turbo compatibility.
    • It is HIGHLY RECOMMENDED to use Turbo when playing RIS due to its many fixes to so many game-breaking bugs (including an enormous increase in loading times) and improvements to RR.
      • Campaigns started with Turbo and vice versa are not compatible with each other!
  • 239 factions, almost all present in the campaign and custom battles.
    • Symbols and banners for all available factions.
    • Every faction has its own starting position, starting army, family tree, treasury, victory conditions and AI personality. Starting armies were rebalanced faction by faction against the faction's income.
    • All factions visible and selectable when starting a new campaign or in the custom battle faction selection screen!
  • Reduced number of settlements from 1800+ to around 1300.
    • New stratmap settlement and port models, as well as many new resource models (some courtesy of Sirrianus Dagovax, Solón de Atenas, and Ariovistus).
    • New campaign map textures and terrain overhaul (as well as the addition of cliffs) based on Makanyane's and wilddog's IWTE base textures.
    • New army and city selection markers inspired by the great work of Solón de Atenas.
    • New campaign map generals and captains
    • Many pathing improvements, and the Nile was made navigable only until Memphis.
    • Sea trade reach and AI sea-invasion distances increased by patching map.rwm.
  • 200+ new remastered units added.
    • Hundreds of new unit descriptions, plus new unit callouts and voice lines.
    • Rome and Italic factions have all new remastered units.
      • Camillan, Polybian, Late Republican units remastered.
      • More than 40 remastered named Roman legions, including all of Caesar's Gallic War legions.
        • Historical battles updated with the remastered units.
    • New Cappadocian, Paphlagonian, Pisidian, Karian, Lykaonian, Kibyrate, Isaurian, Phrygian, Pamphylian and Kaunian units.
    • New Bosporan remastered units with three reforms, including AOR Scythian, Scytho-Bosporan, Taurian, Sindian and Maeotian units.
    • New battlemap captains and generals.
    • New remastered armoured horse models
  • Smaller map with RIS Light and RIS Classic submods, as well as split Rome campaign setups for the smaller map submods as well as the main map.
    • See respective submod for details.
  • Massive battle rebalance and overhauls.
    • Missile combat reworked: accuracy reduced from almost 100%. Added new trajectories and velocities for every projectile, as well as fixing AI units throwing javelins when in mêlée.
    • Attack, charge, mass, armour and shield values reworked, with a new soft armour cap.
    • Elephants and chariots re-tuned (HP, mass, charge and radius).
    • 17+ new skeletons and animations that fix soldier speed and allow a much faster javelin throwing animation sequence.
    • New culture-specific AI formations and battle behaviour.
    • Improvements to the look of vegetation, trees and grass.
    • Battlemap heights rebuilt to match real-world geography more closely.
    • Many, many more improvements and fixes.
  • A new way to recruit units in campaign using recruitment points, which cities accumulate every turn based on what they have built.
    • Higher tier units now no longer locked to higher settlement tiers, but require many recruitment points, so while they would be recruitable in underdevelopped or small settlements, they will take much longer.
    • 148 mercenary pools now covering every region on the map.
    • Cavalry and camel units need horse or camel supply resources.
    • Unit tiers and recruitment time realigned for every roster.
  • Massive number of new building chains (81 total chains, 266 buildings total), with local industry building chains based on the region's resources.
    • The vanilla-style military, temple and hinterland lines are gone, replaced by government, regional-industry, trade and civic chains.
    • Religions consolidated into one temple building. Factional and local conversion are now separate.
    • Many new building icons for the new building chains.
  • Campaign economy overhaul:
    • Every settlement has updated starting population and a full set of pre-built buildings: farms, industry, ports, markets, sanitation and walls.
    • Government public order penalties now depend on the owner's culture versus the local culture. The old 'manufactured' unhappiness for direct and indirect rule and colonies was removed.
    • Empire size reworked: graded tax, law and public-order effects tied to the governor buildings.
    • Growth reworked: health folded into growth, sanitation from town level. Local climate and terrain also affect growth.
    • Resources: a complete new tradable resource set, as well as hidden resources for terrain, climate and landscape.
      • High-tier colony buildings can be limited by landscape.
    • Trade: grain imports and exports with supply points, inland trade centres, market buffs and an empire-size trade nerf. Mines show their real per-mineral income on the building card.
  • Dozens of new traits and ancillaries, going even deeper on character paths with multiple events and decisions taken by the player that will decide the fate of your generals, as well as traits that are passed through new generations;
    • Improvements for Roman cursus honorum traits, triumphs and ovations, as well as a Senate Court trait system;
  • New and improved campaign scripts.
    • Roman, Seleucid, Ptolemaic civil war scripts, including a script for the Roman Social War.
    • Flavour scripts for various factions (festivals), gifts to cities, as well as surrender scripts, spoils of victory, raids, temple sacking, foraging ambushes, army weariness, disease, elephant and ship capture scripts.
    • Re-emergence workaround for dead factions to revive (though Turbo [see above] must be used or there will be CTDs and saves will break when factions revive more than once, or when a faction hordes after it has already died).
    • Abandon settlement and Liberation mechanics for when a settlement does not want to be kept.
    • Various in-game guides behind the '?' button (non-Turbo only).
  • Campaign AI overhaul.
    • Starting relations, alliances, protectorates, personalities rebuilt for all factions based on historical research.
    • New AI recruitment priorities and offsets per unit class. The AI ignores homeland recruitment limits it can't handle, and recruits ships at half cost and in one turn.
    • Naval invasions: the AI ships armies across the Mediterranean, and only factions that should invade by sea prefer it.
  • A great deal of new miscellaneous art
    • New background video for the main menu screen courtesy.
    • New 4K loading screens, as well as new eventpics, and small flourishes to the game's interface for a few cultures.
  • Many new music tracks by Apple, especially for the Romans and Italics, as well as various other factions.
    • The opposing faction's battle music can now also play during mobilisation and combat phases in battle along with the player's.
    • New unit callouts for various units, new scripted event sounds, and many remastered units now have matching vanilla callouts rather than generic ones (e.g. announcing 'silver shields' instead of 'infantry')
  • Partial translation of game files based on vanilla translations.
  • Many, many, many more improvements and fixes.

The team for this version can now be seen at https://rtr-imperium-surrectum.github.io/ris-wiki/team/RIS-Team.html
