Economy overview

In RIS 0.7, economic management has been completely overhauled. Regions and settlements have stronger identities, based on extensive historical research, with more diversity in the way they can develop.

All factions now start the game with a positive income and different paths for settlement development.

Capitals and homeland regions are economically strong, so small factions can support a capable army. Larger factions, by contrast, will need to manage their economies carefully, as new empire size modifiers reduce settlement income as an empire grows.

This page gives an overview of economic management and key strategic considerations. For a detailed look at individual building chains, see the Buildings page.

Strategic Considerations

Know your settlements. The region information scroll shows the factors shaping each settlement, and the building browser shows which building chains are available.

Specialise. Choose governments and building chains that play to each settlement's strengths, as high-tier development of building chains takes considerable investment and trade-offs.

Develop your capital. Capitals are economically powerful in RIS 0.7, thanks to the Homeland government and the treasury building chain, which boosts trade, law and tax.

Preview before you build. To see how a building will affect a settlement, add it to the build queue and check the projected change to income and other figures.

Resources

The map features over 4,000 extensively researched resources, including 42 resource types not found in the base game. As in the base game, each resource has a quantity from 1 to 5, which affects the trade and mining income it generates.

Farming

As in the base game, farming is a key source of both income and growth.

The single vanilla farming chain has been replaced by 10 farming chains. Each settlement has two of them available. Which ones, and how far each can be upgraded, depend on the region's terrain, climate, water sources and other factors.

The choice is usually between:

  • Livestock: geared towards income
  • Crops: geared towards growth

Some farming chains are capped at lower levels in certain settlements, so check the building browser before choosing which to develop.

Trade and Industry

Markets and ports

Markets are now more powerful in inland regions, to balance the strength of coastal ports in the base game. Inland settlements on navigable rivers can also build river ports.

Resource industries

Each settlement can build industries based on the resources in its region. A settlement can build:

  • one rural industry
  • one urban industry
  • one heavy industry

Resource-rich regions may offer several options in each category, so plan accordingly.

Heavy industries come with significant trade-offs, so they are usually best kept for well-developed settlements. Early in the game, and in smaller settlements, rural and urban industries may be the better choice.

Grain Import and Export

Grain import and export is a new mechanic that balances growth between regions using grain surplus points.

  • Grain export buildings reduce a settlement's growth and produce faction-wide grain surplus points.
  • Grain import buildings consume faction-wide grain surplus points and give the settlement extra happiness and growth.

The region information scroll in your capital shows how many grain surplus points your faction is producing and consuming.

Resource Export/Supply

Some resource exploitation building chains include a special export/supply level at the end of their chain that offer unique strategic bonuses. You can only build one of each type across your realm. Effects on other buildings will, in most cases, also be fulfilled by the resource being available locally, in which case the bonuses will not stack if you also have an export level built in your faction. These buildings will specify in their effects what regions they affect. (Faction) mean it affects all regions factionwide, or all specified buildings factionwide, (Capital) means it only affects your capital. Controlling and developing key settlements is essential to unlocking these powerful features:

Lead: +5% health bonus to all sanitation (tier 3-5) buildings, tax bonus for all Metalworkers.

Wine: +5% happiness factionwide.

Tin: +10% trade bonus factionwide, +10% trade to all Metalworkers (stacks with copper).

Copper: +10% trade to Metalworkers factionwide (stacks with tin), +1 armour for all blacksmiths (does not stack with other resources), tax bonus for all mints (central and local).

Iron: Enables Weaponsmith and Metalworkers construction anywhere, +1 weapon and +1 armour for all weaponsmiths (does not stack with other resources, except for weapons with coal), tax bonus for all Metalworkers (does not stack with lead).

Purple Dye: +5% law bonus to all cities (minor, large, huge), 10% trade bonus for all textiles buildings (tier 2-3, stacks with dyes on tier 3).

Dyes: 10% trade bonus for all textiles buildings (tier 2-3, stacks with purple dye on tier 3).

Marble: 10% construction cost reduction for all temples, 10% construction cost reduction for civic buildings in your capital.

Horses: Enables factional heavy cavalry recruitment factionwide, enables races on all racing & athletics buildings, enables upgrading racing & athletics buildings anywhere (tier 3-4).

Camels: Enables factional camel units production factionwide, enables races on all racing & athletics buildings (tier 1 some factions, tier 2 all factions).

Papyrus: law bonus for all law courts (tier 2-3) and academies (tier 2-3) (does not stack with parchment).

Livestock (parchment): law bonus for all law courts (tier 2-3) and academies (tier 2-3) (does not stack with papyrus).

Salt: +10% trade for all (tier 2-5) animal husbandry farms, +10% trade for all fish salting buildings, enables tier 3 fish salting buildings (some factions), and +2% tax bonus factionwide.

Silk: +10% trade bonus factionwide.

Incense: +10% happiness factionwide.

Amber: +10% culture conversion bonus factionwide.

Medicinal herbs & Perfumes: +5% health to all healers (tier 2-3, can stack with other resources on tier 3).

Wild Animals: +5% happiness bonus factionwide, bonus happiness for all arena's (Romans).

Pitch, Bitumen & Tars: +1 attack for ships on all ports (can stack with timber and hemp).

Hemp: +1 attack for ships on all ports (can stack with timber and pitch).

Timber: +1 attack for ships on all ports (can stack with pitch and hemp), -1% construction cost for most civilian buildings.

Slave Trade: +0.5% growth in homeland regions, tax bonus for all crop farms (tier 4-5, 3 for qanat), all mines, all stone quarries, all marble quarries, all salt exploitation, all sulphur exploitation, bonus happiness for all arena's (Romans), but -5% happiness factionwide.

Sulphur: +1 farming for cropfarms (tier 3-5, does not stack with grain/elephants).

Stone: +1 construction point in your capital, but also -10% tax in capital.

Honey: +2 health (10% happiness and 1% growth) in your capital.

Jewellery: +20% trade bonus in your capital.

Olive Oil: +1% population growth bonus in your capital.

Spices: +20% trade bonus in your capital.
