Ardiaei: Illyrian Satisfaction Is Not Easy

For: Ardiaei · By: nerva6569

A few tips and tricks to play as the Ardiaei. Might contain useful information to play as any South Illyrian factions.

Overview

The Ardiaei stand at the crossroads of Europe, and the major civilizations of the Mediterranean loom on the horizon. Across the Adriatic, the Roman juggernaut is awakening, having consolidated most of the Italic Peninsula south of the Po. To the south, your allies, the Illyrian Kingdom and the Labeatae, are all that stand between you and the powerful Hellenistic monarchies of the Antigonid Kingdom and Epirus. To the north, a new threat rises following the Celtic migrations: the Taurisci, the Eravisci and the Scordisci may all choose to encroach on the region when you least expect them.

At the start of the game, in 270 BCE, the Ardiaei are a small and poor tribe that controls only two regions. Your starting diplomatic situation will temporarily secure your southern border; the Illyrian Kingdom and the Labeatae, your Illyrian cousins to the south, should not bother you for several turns. To the northwest, the Daesitiates and Delmatae govern their own poorly developed towns; they may prove a threat to your flank if you fail to guard your borders. To the west lies the commonwealth of Greek towns controlled by Issa. Issa will attack you; there is nothing you can do about it. They will blockade your ports by the second turn and will probably be the first enemy you have to deal with.

Illyria is a difficult region to control: most provinces will not have any roads, will not provide much income, and will contain plenty of rugged terrain. You'll easily find yourself at war with all your neighbors at the same time. You can only count on the Adriatic Sea as an effective barrier; the Romans will normally only invade from the sea in the south (around the Apollonia-Epeiros settlement).

I think a sensible defensive line for the first turns of the campaign, one that makes the best use of river crossings and mountain passes, runs through Skodra, Meteon, Rhizon, Narenson Teichos (not ideal, but a Homeland region worth defending), Delmium, Argyruntum, and Idassa. By the time you control these settlements, a 5-6 unit army (preferably cavalry, but some cheap infantry could be a lighter burden on your budget), plus a general with traits that increase movement points, should be enough to fend off attacks from the north and northeast. Keep a fleet in tow at all times; in many cases, it can transport your patrol army much faster by sea.

Getting Started

You can rush Narona with Southern Illyrian Cavalry, Plator the Nestaian and Pleuratos the Ardiaean, plus ideally a unit of mercenary infantry (I chose Southern Illyrian Spearmen). There is no need to assault the town; the garrison will sally out during the Free Peoples' turn. It is a difficult battle, but do your best to defeat them. The rest of your army can start besieging Daorson and building battering rams. You may assault it on turn two with the aid of your general (Plator the Nestaian or Pleuratos the Ardiaean).

Build a Tannery in Rhizon and Slope Pasturing in Narenson Teichos. These might help with your initial income struggles.

Recruit a Spy as soon as possible. Due to the forested nature of Illyria, there is a higher chance of being ambushed if you send your armies into the hinterlands without proper scouting.

As mentioned before, Issa will most likely attack you by the second turn. Your starting army should be enough to push them back into the sea. Avoid engaging Issa's fleet with your own; Illyrian Boats are inferior to any Greek vessel.

The Delmatae will come knocking soon enough. Taking their capital of Delmium should easily cripple the tribe's ability to recruit troops against you. The Delmatae provinces will prove valuable to your nation in the future due to their mineral riches.

Tips

Economic Tips

  • Your starting regions and the ones around them are quite poor; on top of that, the Illyrian culture group is blocked from building Sanitation or Healing buildings beyond the second level. Developing a City is possible, but reaching Large City or even dreaming of reaching Huge City levels will require special micromanagement on your part. Be sure to prioritize your competent governors accordingly.
  • The Delmatae provinces should be developed as fast as possible. Avoid destroying their population upon conquest.
  • Delmium possesses Copper, Stone, and Gold. Ideally, develop the Stone Quarry building tree to cheaply build more infrastructure and prioritize population growth buildings. Once you can grow the settlement into a city, destroy the Stone Quarry building and switch to Gold mining. A gold-producing province with a Deep Mining Complex (Industry) and Direct Rule buildings will probably provide more income than several of your other Illyrian provinces combined.
  • Promona possesses Iron deposits. However, developing a full Iron Export operation will prove difficult. You may instead choose to grow the settlement into a Large Town and use its Iron deposits to upgrade your armies' armour.
  • This is not an easy region, nor an easy faction in which to develop your economy. You may have to use unconventional tactics like selling Map Information around or sacking and completely razing all buildings in settlements you are not interested in keeping.
  • Hint: The city of Apollonia-Epeiros, just to your south, may be vulnerable to one of these thorough sackings due to its size and the difficulty of holding it at the beginning of your campaign.
  • It might not be the brightest idea (especially if you are playing the Four-Romans campaign), but razing Italian settlements on the Adriatic coast and abandoning them could become quite a profitable venture, and might even help slow down the House of Aemilii and the House of Claudii from encroaching into your lands (Four Romans Campaign).

Military Tips

  • The Ardiaei are the northernmost Illyrian nation with the ability to recruit heavy shock cavalry from turn one, in the form of the Illyrian Noble Cavalry. They will outmatch any mounted units in melee that the Delmatae, Issa, or the Daesitiates can field, and will prove useful for charging directly at skirmishers.
  • Don't sleep on the Southern Illyrian Levy unit; they are quite feeble, but possess a few advantages that make them a valuable asset in the warfare style of the Illyrian-Pannonian-Dalmatian region:
  • High numbers: their battalions are 50% bigger compared to other available infantry options and can easily bog down better opposition, soak up cavalry charges or absorb enemy missiles.
  • Plenty of ammunition: each soldier carries 5 javelins; despite their low damage compared to other units, the sheer number can decimate the opposition's light units or tear into the flank or rear of better-armoured units. In the region, only a few units will escape a Levy volley unscathed.
  • Great availability: you can retrain them across a large area, as they are found in an Area of Recruitment that stretches from the borders of Macedonia and Epirus all the way to the Istrian Peninsula. They can be recruited with only a Garrison Building or Tier 1 Military Industrial Complex, under any form of government. The unit's high degree of replaceability and low price will allow you to use them as meat shields without much thought.
  • Flexibility: this is not an amazing unit, but it can perform well if used correctly against both skirmishers and heavier infantry. Rain death on enemy skirmishers or use their speed to avoid heavy infantry. They can be used as garrisons, to hold your line or for skirmishing. The Southern Illyrian Spearmen might appear superior; however, their lack of javelins and still-poor defensive stats will see them outmatched in open battle against the numerous javelin throwers you will encounter in the area.
  • Issaian AoR units will come in quite handy when dealing with heavier opposition or assaulting towns.
  • Issaian Heavy Infantry will be the heaviest infantry available in your area in the short term, until you get access to the Illyrian Epilektoi after reaching your reforms. You'll be able to recruit them easily by taking Issa and building a Dependency government building. Make sure to evaluate the trade-off of administering Issa as a Dependency, as it might significantly hammer down the revenue it generates.
  • Issaian Epibatai are far superior to your native Slave Slingers. A unit or two of them will greatly improve your long-range skirmishing options.
  • Thureophoroi will become available for AoR recruitment in the Greek (Doric) towns of the Illyrian coast after the global Greek reform. Thureophoroi might be easily bested on the battlefields of Greece and Italy, but in Illyria, they reign supreme: mobile, with a ranged attack, decent defense, and able to mow down lighter cavalry; your Illyrian neighbors will have few comparable units.
  • Other Illyrian AoR units:
  • Once the "Second wave of military innovations have arrived!" reform hits, two Thureophoroi units will become available that represent an upgrade over the traditional Greek Thureophoroi: the Liburnian Thureophoroi on the Adriatic coast (see AoR) and the Dalmatian Thureophoroi further inland. Both are solid line infantry, but the latter suffers from a limited area of recruitment and might become difficult to replenish. The Liburnian version, while more readily available, will force you to keep a Dependency government in one or more profitable settlements.
