Empire size

Empire size counts the settlements a faction holds. Every settlement counts as one, whatever its size or wealth. The count puts the faction on one of 10 levels, and each level changes taxes, trade income and public order in every settlement the faction holds. The map has far more settlements than the game's own corruption was made for; empire size keeps large factions from snowballing.

  • Small factions get extra taxes and a little extra law, most of the tax in their capital.
  • Growing factions lose those bonuses step by step.
  • Large empires pay ever more in taxes and trade income outside their capital.

Taking a settlement adds one to the count, losing one takes one off. When the count crosses a boundary the faction moves up or down a level, so an empire that shrinks gets its lower level's modifiers back.

AI expansion bonus

Under the AI, Rome (and in Four Romans the Houses of Aemilii and Cornelii), the Parni, Carthage, the Aetolian League, the Achaean League and Pontus also get, in every settlement at levels 1–5:

  • taxable income +5 at level 1, +4 at level 2, +3 at level 3, +2 at level 4, +1 at level 5
  • recruits gain 1 experience
  • 1 extra construction point
