The Hellenistic Rebellions

Main campaignRIS LightRIS Classic

Breaks away: Seleucid Rebels (Upper Satrapies) from The Seleucid Empire

Connected rebellions split the Seleucid Empire. The Seleucid Dynasts, Adaios' breakaway Thracian satrapy, are not raised here, but their fate is settled here whenever they are alive; from turn 20 (266 BC) Hyrkania and Parthia break away from the Seleucid Empire as the Seleucid Satraps. Separately, Cyrene rejoins the Ptolemaic Empire when its faction leader is replaced, the death of Magas in the messages.

Any campaign, whether the empires are human or AI: the Seleucid Dynasts' rules apply whenever they are alive, the Seleucid Satraps need the Seleucid Empire to hold at least one of their towns, and Cyrene's return needs a campaign not played as Cyrene.

What sets it off

  • The Seleucid Dynasts are not created by this section; everything below about them applies from their first turn alive.
  • At the start of the Seleucid Empire's first turn from turn 20 (266 BC), each of Zadrakarta, Hekatompylos and Nisa that the Seleucid Empire still holds revolts, and the Seleucid Satraps (shown as Seleucid Rebels) emerge there.
  • The eastern revolt is tried only once, and never if the Seleucid succession revolt has already happened, if the Seleucid Satraps already exist, or if the Seleucid Empire holds none of those towns at that moment.
  • When Cyrene gets a new faction leader from turn 4 (270 BC) onwards, Cyrene rejoins the Ptolemaic Empire at the start of the next faction's turn, provided no human plays Cyrene and Cyrene and the Ptolemaic Empire are both still alive; otherwise it never happens.

What happens

  • On the Seleucid Dynasts' first turn they take their reserved holding settlement if the Free Peoples hold it.
  • At the start of their turn, until it has been done once, one of the Seleucid Dynasts' family generals, preferably one who is neither faction leader nor heir, is moved to a fixed spot and made immovable and immortal; this is skipped if one of their characters is already immovable.
  • A human Seleucid Empire gets the message 'AN UPSTART DYNAST' at the start of their first turn on which the Seleucid Dynasts are alive and at war with them.
  • The Seleucid Dynasts give up once the Seleucid Empire has won more than 2 land battles against them, once they get a new faction leader from turn 4 (270 BC) onwards, or once they hold fewer than 2 settlements, their reserved holding settlement included.
  • When a human plays neither the Seleucid Empire nor the Seleucid Dynasts, this happens automatically at the start of the next faction's turn while both are alive: they take back their reserved holding settlement and every other Dynasts settlement passes to the Seleucid Empire.
  • A human Seleucid Empire is instead asked once, at the start of a turn while still at war with the Dynasts, through the message 'The Thracian Satrapy Submits': accepting takes back every Dynasts city except their reserved holding settlement, each with a government building, and declining keeps the war going with no further offer.
  • When the Seleucid Dynasts give up, their faction leader, or failing him another family general, is moved to a fixed spot and made immovable and immortal, all their armies except those of immovable generals are destroyed, and they become neutral with the Seleucid Empire.
  • If those dormant Seleucid Dynasts later hold more than one settlement, one of their immovable generals loses both traits and is moved to a fixed spot.
  • Human Seleucid Dynasts are never made to give up.
  • In the east, the rebelling towns pass to the Seleucid Satraps, which did not exist until now, with garrisons drawn from Seleucid Chalkaspides, Thureophoroi, Greek Hoplites, Greek Peltasts, Greek Archers, Seleucid Xystophoroi, Prodromoi and Neocretan Archers.
  • On the Satraps' first turn they take their reserved holding settlement if the Free Peoples hold it, and every one of the three towns held by the Seleucid Empire or the Free Peoples also passes to them, as does every one held by the Parni or Bactria unless a human plays that faction.
  • At the same moment the Parni, unless a human plays them, lose every settlement they hold to the Free Peoples.
  • If Bactria and the Seleucid Empire are both alive, the Seleucid Empire and Bactria are put at war.
  • The Satraps are put at war with the Parni, and while the Parni live the Satraps are put back at war with them at the start of each of their turns, except while they lie dormant after giving up.
  • Like the Dynasts, the Satraps have one family general, preferably neither faction leader nor heir, moved to a fixed spot and made immovable and immortal at the start of their turn, until it has been done once, unless one of their characters is already immovable.
  • When a human plays neither the Satraps nor the Parni, the Satraps give up automatically once they have played at least one turn and either get a new faction leader, hold fewer than 2 settlements, their reserved holding settlement included, or have run up more than 2 counted losses.
  • Losses are counted at the start of each of their turns, and the count is wiped whenever one of their generals captures a settlement: if they held 4 or more settlements, their reserved holding settlement included, at the start of their previous turn and have captured none since, holding 4 or fewer now counts 3 losses at once; a drop to a single settlement also counts, but that alone already makes them give up, and a drop from 3 settlements to 2 counts nothing.
  • This count is also taken on their first turn, so Satraps that emerge with exactly 4 settlements give up after that turn unless one of their generals captures a settlement during it.
  • They then take back their reserved holding settlement, and their other settlements go to the Parni if they are alive, otherwise to the Seleucid Empire if it is alive, with a government building in each for a human Seleucid player, otherwise to the Free Peoples.
  • A human Parni player is instead asked once, through the message 'The Satraps Submit', when the same conditions are met: accepting gives the Parni every Satraps city except their reserved holding settlement, each with a government building, and declining keeps the war going with no further offer.
  • When the Satraps give up, their faction leader, or failing him another family general, is moved to a fixed spot and made immovable and immortal, all their armies except those of immovable generals are destroyed, and they become neutral with the Parni and the Seleucid Empire.
  • If those dormant Satraps later hold more than one settlement, one of their immovable generals loses both traits and is moved to a fixed spot.
  • Human Seleucid Satraps are never made to give up.
  • When Cyrene rejoins the Ptolemaic Empire, every Cyrene settlement passes to the Ptolemaic Empire, with a government building in each for a human Ptolemaic player, and every Cyrene army is destroyed.
  • A human Ptolemaic player then gets the message 'The Death of Magas' at the start of their turn.
  • Each rebellion, surrender and return from dormancy happens at most once per campaign.

Playing as the rebels

A human Seleucid Empire player is told of Adaios' revolt through the message 'AN UPSTART DYNAST' at the start of their first turn while the Seleucid Dynasts are at war with them; accepting switches the player to the Seleucid Dynasts at the start of the next turn, if the Dynasts still hold a settlement, with a government building in each of their settlements except their reserved holding settlement. The same player is offered the Seleucid Satraps through the message 'Chaos in the East!' at the start of the turn their revolt is set off; accepting switches the player to the Satraps at the start of the next turn on which the Satraps hold a settlement, with a government building in each of their settlements except their reserved holding settlement, and their faction leader is made free to move again if he was immovable. When the Seleucid succession revolt breaks out, the same player is offered the Seleucid Dynasts at the start of their next turn ('Asia Minor Secedes from the Empire!') while the Dynasts hold a settlement, and if that offer is declined or not made, the Seleucid Satraps ('The Upper Satrapies revolt!') while the Satraps hold a settlement. A human Ptolemaic Empire player is offered the Ptolemaic Dynasts at the start of the turn after the Ptolemaic succession revolt breaks out ('War of the Brothers') while the Dynasts hold a settlement. Accepting any of the other offers places a government building in every settlement of that faction except its reserved holding settlement and switches the player to it at once; declining keeps the player where they are and leaves the rebels to the AI. Human Parni and Bactria players only get 'Chaos in the East!' as information, and no one is offered Cyrene.

The in-game messages (10)

AN UPSTART DYNAST

Adaios, your satrap of Thrace, thinks with the backing of his holdings he can simply revolt! Show him why some backwater barbarians aren't enough to challenge you!

(Accept to switch to the Seleucid Dynasts next turn or Deny to remain with the Seleucids)

The Thracian Satrapy Submits

The revolt in Thrace is broken, the armies of the traitors have been defeated and little remains of their power. The officers of the Seleucid Dynasts offer to hand back every city they still hold if their lives are spared. Should we accept their surrender offer and take back those cities peacefully or should we Deny to fight on and punish these traitors?

Chaos in the East!

Due to distance, and succession disputes within the Seleucid Empire, the Satraps of Bactria, Hyrkania, and Parthia have all claimed their independence! Meanwhile, there are rumours of the Parni, one of the Dahaean tribes setting their sights once more on their now weakened southern neighbours.

(Accept to switch to the Seleucid Satraps next turn. Deny to remain with the Seleucids)

Chaos in the East!

Due to distance, and succession disputes within the Seleucid Empire, the Satraps of Bactria, Hyrkania, and Parthia have all claimed their independence! Meanwhile, there are rumours of the Parni, one of the Dahaean tribes setting their sights once more on their now weakened southern neighbours.

The Satraps Submit

The breakaway Satraps of Hyrkania and Parthia are beaten, their armies scattered to the winds and their main leaders dead. What is left of their realm has now been offered to us if their men are allowed to return home unarmed.

Should we accept their surrender offer and take back those cities peacefully or should we deny to fight on and punish their betrayal?

Return of the Lysimachids?

A member of your royal house who is a descendent of Lysimachos, your former rival, has conspired and revolted with the tyrant of Miletos! He seeks to re-gain his father's former possessions and glory!

(Accept to take command of the Ptolemaic Dynasts or Deny to remain with the Ptolemies)

The Death of Magas

Magas, the general who revolted from the Ptolemaic Empire and sought to forge his own dynasty, has died, leaving no heir to claim his Kingdom. Because of this, the land of the Kingdom of Cyrene has peacefully returned to the Ptolemaic Kingdom.

Asia Minor Secedes from the Empire!

With the death of our Leader, a rival family member has decided he should be Basileus and has taken all of the Seleucid territory in Asia Minor for himself!

(Accept to take command of the Seleucid Dynasts or Deny to remain with the Seleucids)

The Upper Satrapies revolt!

With the recent succession crisis in the Seleucid Empire, multiple generals in the Upper Satrapies have decided to break away from the Seleucid Empire and declare their sovereignty over the lands they own. They no longer answer to us and refuse to pay tribute. We need to gather our armies and march on them. These traitors will be punished for their insolence!

(Accept to take command of the Seleucid Satraps or Deny to remain with the Seleucids)

War of the Brothers

Your brother has decided he should be Pharaoh and not you, because of this, he's split away from your Kingdom, taking all of Cyrene with him!

(Accept to take command of the Ptolemaic Dynasts or Deny to remain with the Ptolemies)
