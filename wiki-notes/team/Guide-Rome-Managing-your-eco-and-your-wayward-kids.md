In 270 BC, Rome stands as the dominant power on the Italian peninsula. Having largely consolidated central and southern Italy, your immediate borders are secure save for the islands and Cisalpine Gaul to the north. A few independent Italic states still hold out, while several others begin the campaign as Socii (protectorates) under your banner.

**Starting Economy, Military and Diplomacy:**
* **Military:** Very Strong. You begin with two massive field armies (a 20-stack and a 19-stack), alongside scattered garrisons that can be consolidated into a third defensive force.
* **Economy:** Weak. Despite a healthy starting treasury of 22,500 denarii, your net income sits at a razor-thin +1,866 denarii per turn before tax adjustments, making early expansion or careful upkeep, tax and building management a top priority.
* **Diplomacy:** Very middling. Most neighboring factions either dislike you or feel neutral at best. Your protectorates are also looking for any opportunity to break away if they smell weakness or if your diplomatic reputation drops. That said, they provide a massive chunk of extra tribute in those critical starting turns, amounting to nearly 10,000 to 15,000 denarii per turn if you're lucky.

**Expansion & Campaign Pacing:**
Sitting at the center of the Mediterranean, Rome has total freedom to expand in any direction, whether pushing north, south, west across the islands, or east into the Balkans. However, this central position also means managing multiple borders and potential flashpoints at once. Overall, the campaign begins as a balancing act where you command a powerful military while walking a financial and diplomatic tightrope. Once you stabilize your income and secure your frontiers, Rome gradually snowballs into an unstoppable empire.

**Campaign Feel:**
Methodical and multi-theater. Unlike smaller factions fighting for survival from a handful of settlements, Rome plays like a rising superpower managing constant border friction, delicate protectorate politics, and high-stakes wars on multiple fronts at once.

## **Getting started**

**Early-Game Approaches:**
You have a lot of freedom in the opening turns. Rome can play tall while you sort out your economic and diplomatic situation, or you can play wide and conquer your way out of your tight budget and awkward border situation. Regardless of your approach, you can also disband redundant garrisons and navy to save on immediate upkeep.

**Key Targets for Early Expansion:**
* **Asculum & Ankon (Central/Northern Italy):** Asculum is an Italic settlement held by the independent Picentes. Crushing them eliminates an independent state on your doorstep and opens the path north toward Ankon, a rebel-held Dorian settlement with Greek AoR units.
* **Rhegium & Messana (The Southern Strait):** Your southern army is poised to strike at Rhegium, a rebel-held Ionian settlement with Greek AoR units. Taking it lets you cross straight into Messana, held by the Ionian (Campanian) Mamertines, giving you a secure foothold in Sicily and a recruitment hub for Campanian units.
* **Sicily & Beyond:** Once established in Messana, you are in position to push further into Sicily against Syracuse, Acragas, and likely your first major rival: Carthage.

**Managing the Economy & Infrastructure:**
Because Rome starts with a tight net income, choosing where to invest your 22,500 treasury makes a huge difference. Rather than following a strict build order, it helps to prioritize infrastructure based on what each settlement offers:
* **Farms & Local Resources:** Farms are typically your best overall investment. Beyond increasing population growth and base agricultural income, farms can sometimes unlock extra trade bonuses in settlements with specific local resources, such as fruits, olives, grapes, papyrus, cotton, flax, or dates. Rome itself benefits directly from this right at the start.
* **Ports & Trade Routes:** With Italy's extensive coastline, ports and paved roads offer reliable, scaling trade income, especially if you maintain trade agreements with nearby neutral states. A high-level port can easily net you 500 extra income per turn, and roads also pay you back with faster troop movement.
* **Taxes vs. Growth:** Raising taxes provides quick cash to offset army upkeep, though lower taxes encourage faster population growth, helping your settlements reach higher tiers and replenish Roman manpower sooner. RIS 0.7 has made it so slavery is optional to grow your cities. With some effort, you can get all your cities to the huge city level using a mix of grain imports and a governor with the appropriate population growth and squalor reduction traits and followers.
* **Protect Your Tribute:** Keeping your diplomatic standing solid ensures your Socii protectorates' loyalty and keeps them paying their massive tribute while your domestic economy matures.



**Neighboring Factions & Diplomacy:**

Rome sits at a diplomatic crossroads, with its heartland dotted by protectorates and bordered by minor holdouts and major Mediterranean powers. Managing these relationships determines how many fronts you have to fight on at once:
* **The Socii (Italian Protectorates):** Your scattered Italian allies provide vital buffer zones and up to 10,000 to 15,000 denarii per turn in tribute. You can nurture these alliances to fund your early empire, or cancel your protectorate status with them later for direct control over their cities. Keep your diplomatic reputation intact, as any sign of weakness can tempt them to break away. Also watch out for them signing alliances with independent factions like the Messapians, Apuani, and Sarsinates, as your protectorates will break away if you declare war on any of their allies.
* **Independent Mainland & Sicilian States:** Factions like the Picentes and Messapians on the mainland, or Syracuse, Acragas, and the Mamertines on Sicily, sit right along your natural expansion routes. Mainland holdouts are easy targets to clean up your borders, while Sicilian cities can serve as brief trading partners before you push deeper into the island.
* **Cisalpine Gaul (The Northern Tribes):** The Gallic tribes to your north are aggressive neighbors sitting on wealthy agricultural land. You can either maintain defensive garrisons along your starting borders to keep them contained, or push north early while some tribes only hold a single settlement, securing the Po Valley before they build up troublesome stacks.
* **Carthage:** Holding western Sicily along with parts of Sardinia and Corsica, Carthage is your most dangerous immediate rival. Stepping onto Sicily will almost certainly trigger a major war, so you can choose to strike while your starting armies are strong or stall for time in Messana until you build a proper economy, army, and fleet.
* **Epirus & The Balkans:** Across the Adriatic, Epirus and the Illyrian tribes rarely bother invading the Italian coast directly. Instead, they serve as convenient early trading partners to help boost your port income, leaving your eastern flank quiet until you are ready to expand across the sea.

## **Military & Reforms**

Rome begins the campaign with one of the strongest early-game armies in the Mediterranean, though managing your manpower and knowing when your roster evolves is key to sustaining long campaigns:
* **The Camillan Roster:** At 270 BC, you field Camillan-era legions. Early Hastati, Principes, Triarii, Equites, and skirmishers such as Leves and Rorarii give you a disciplined, flexible frontline that comfortably holds its own against both Gallic warbands and Greek phalanxes.
* **Manpower & Area of Recruitment (AoR):** Because core Roman units can only be recruited and retrained in your Italian heartland, you cannot replenish citizen losses on the front lines. Mixing in regional AoR units, such as Greek troops from Italy's east coast or Campanians from Capua, Neapolis, and Messana, lets you keep campaigning while rotating battered Roman units back home to retrain. These local units are also much easier to replenish on the fly if you are fighting in Sicily through Messana (Campanian) or Rhegium (Greek).
* **The 71-Settlement Reform:** Rome's next major military reform unlocks once you directly hold 71 settlements. Geographically, this equals roughly all of mainland Italy (including your starting protectorates), Sicily, Sardinia, Corsica, and a few settlements of the Po Valley. Whether you choose to push straight for that milestone to upgrade your legions or expand at a slower pace is entirely up to you.

**Roster Strengths, Weaknesses & Combat Tips:**
While Roman heavy infantry is superb, early Roman cavalry (Equites) is underwhelming to say the least. If you want stronger mounted options early on, consider recruiting regional cavalry from your Italic conquests, such as Messapian or Campanian horsemen. Also, do not be fooled by the raw attack stats on enemy spearmen. While spearmen get a bonus against cavalry, they suffer a hidden -4 penalty against infantry, meaning your Principes will reliably beat spearmen with equal or even higher stats in a straight fight.


## **Tips**

* **Deploy Diplomats on Turn 1:** Recruit 3 diplomats on Turn 1 to sell trade rights and map information for extra cash. Consider offering alliances to your northern neighbors, the Apuani and Sarsinates, to keep them docile while you build a garrison fort between Pisae and Arretium to guard that border.
* **Optimize Followers & Governors Early:** Place a governor in every settlement when possible, putting your best characters in high-income cities or growth targets. Early on, stack tax, trade, population growth, and squalor-reduction followers onto your governor in Rome. A strong Turn 1 move is moving young Crispus from Fregellae to Rome and transferring Gallus's Scribe (trade income) to him.
* **Speed-Run Huge Cities with Grain Imports:** Use grain imports to push key cities like Rome to the Huge City tier quickly. Once a city reaches Huge City status, you can remove the grain imports, crank taxes up to Very High for massive income (even if the population shrinks slightly), and move your growth governor and followers on to the next city.
* **Prioritize All Population Growth Buildings:** Beyond basic farms, look for healing and water buildings, Grape Orchards, Olive Groves, Fish Salters, Fruit Orchards, and Milling Districts. Even a Level 3 Forum provides a 0.5% population growth bonus alongside unlocking higher building tiers.
* **Audit Your Infrastructure & Farms:** Check the building browser in both starting and conquered settlements. You can demolish redundant Local Garrisons, Barracks, and Temples for immediate cash and higher tax income. Later on, switching suboptimal farms from husbandry to crops can net you noticeably better population growth.
* **Manage Corruption as You Expand:** While Local Garrison buildings reduce tax income in core provinces, they provide Law bonuses (alongside walls and colonies) that fight corruption. Build them in distant conquests far from Rome where corruption losses outweigh the tax penalty.
* **Know Your Italic Unit Logistics:** Unlike standard Roman Equites, Italic Equites are actually good. You can retrain your starting Italic Hastati and Principes in Praeneste (Latium, east of Rome), though it is smart to eventually phase them out for native Roman variants, which can retrain anywhere with Colony 2 and MIC 2.

## **Mistakes to avoid**

* **Converting Government Buildings on Turn 1:** Your starting Dependencies buildings are not optimal, and you will eventually want to swap most of them to Direct Rule once you run out of economic building slots. However, due to the high cost and slow return on investment, do not waste money converting them on Turn 1.
* **Ignoring Your Protectorates Too Long:** Do not leave your Socii protectorates unchecked forever. Ending those treaties early on your own terms is much safer than getting backstabbed while your armies are tied down in Sicily or the Po Valley.
* **Leading Armies with Poor-Trait Generals:** Be careful with generals who carry negative traits beyond simple morale penalties. Traits like "Unreliable" can reduce your chances of triggering positive loot events after battles or even trigger negative ones.
* **Assuming Conquered Settlements Are Built Optimally:** Do not leave newly captured cities on autopilot. Always inspect their existing farms and buildings in the browser, as the AI frequently builds suboptimal chains that hold back your growth and income.
